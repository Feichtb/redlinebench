# Architectural Document Review: Bonfire House

## Step 1: Project Context

**Project:** Bonfire House, single-family residence  
**Location:** Chatham County, North Carolina  
**Code:** 2018 North Carolina Residential Code (IRC 2015 with amendments)  
**Building type:** R-3 occupancy, Construction Type V-B  
**Climate zone:** 4A (mixed-humid)  
**Drawing scope:** Complete architectural set (34 sheets: general, floor plans, elevations, sections, details, schedules). Structural, MEP, and civil engineering disciplines referenced but not included.  
**Specifications:** 11-page project specification document provided covering materials, systems, and construction requirements.

## Summary

The Bonfire House drawing set and specifications represent a comprehensive residential design package with strong architectural intent and modern energy-efficient features. However, the documents contain **87 identified issues** requiring resolution before construction, with concerns distributed across all review categories.

**Top three areas of greatest concern:**

1. **Code compliance verification gaps** (Category E): Critical dimensional information is missing for code verification, including stair geometry that as-drawn violates riser height maximums, unverified egress window compliance for sleeping rooms, missing guardrail heights meeting code minimums, and absent life-safety device locations (smoke alarms, bathroom ventilation). These items will generate immediate plan review comments from the building department.

2. **Incomplete construction dimensions** (Category B): Extensive missing dimensions create ambiguity for contractors, including stair geometry, window sill heights, ceiling heights in sloped areas, cabinet dimensions, handrail and guardrail details, hallway widths, bathroom fixture clearances, and foundation wall heights. The lack of dimensioned information will generate numerous RFIs and potential field conflicts.

3. **Building envelope detailing** (Category D): Several critical weatherproofing and thermal control details are incomplete or missing, including roof ventilation intake at soffits, continuous insulation transitions at overhangs, window head flashing, deck ledger flashing at building connection, and foundation insulation depth. These gaps present moisture intrusion and thermal performance risks in the mixed-humid climate of North Carolina.

The documents would benefit from a thorough coordination review focusing on dimensional completeness, code compliance verification information, and building envelope detailing before issuing for construction.
