# CONSTRUCTION DOCUMENT REVIEW — BONFIRE HOUSE (BID SET, 10/20/2021)

## Step 1: Project Context

- **Project:** Bonfire House, un-addressed parcel, Chatham County, North Carolina. Jurisdiction: Chatham County Building Inspections / Central Permitting. Code: 2018 North Carolina Residential Code (adopting IRC 2015 with amendments).
- **Building type:** New single-family dwelling, R-3 occupancy; drawings state Type V-B construction (an IBC classification, not used by the IRC). Two stories over a 6'-0" sealed crawl space, attached garage, above-ground lap pool, exterior wood deck.
- **Climate zone:** 4A (stated on G0.11; consistent with Chatham County, NC).
- **Scope of set:** Architectural only — G0.01 (symbols/abbreviations), G0.11 (code summary), G1.00 (renderings), A1.00 (survey, removed), A1.01 (site), A2.00–A2.03 (plans), A3.01–A3.03 (elevations), A3.10–A3.22 (sections/enlarged elevations), A4.00–A4.13 (assemblies and exterior details), A5.01–A5.30 (deck/pool), A6.01 (RCP), A7.01–A7.10 (interior elevations/details), A8.01 (schedules). **No structural, mechanical, plumbing, electrical, civil, landscape, or life-safety sheets are included** — repeatedly referenced as "see structural drawings," "bidder design," and "per code." Their absence is noted as context only, not as a finding.
- **Specifications:** An 11-page narrative "Description of Materials, Applications and Allowances," not CSI-formatted and containing no division numbers or section numbers. Drawing references to "SPECIFICATIONS DIV. 8" (G0.01) therefore cannot resolve to anything in the project manual.

## Summary

The set is graphically well organized and unusually thorough for a single-family project in its assembly and detail development, but it is not yet coordinated to bid quality. The most serious problems are **conflicts between the drawings and the narrative specification** — different window manufacturers, different wall insulation systems and R-values, contradictory cladding locations (the west facade is assigned two materials), an EV charger shown as an outlet and specified as hard-wired, and a solar array drawn in full while the specification only requires the building to be "solar ready." Second, **wet-area and envelope detailing has substantive technical gaps**: the shower floor assembly has no waterproofing membrane, large-format stone is thin-set directly on plywood, no window sill pans are detailed, floor drains appear in wood-framed floors with no supporting assembly, and a snap-lock standing seam roof is specified on 2:12 and 1/4:12 planes. Third, **life-safety information is largely absent**: no emergency escape openings, safety glazing, smoke/CO alarms, handrails, or garage fire-separation assemblies are identified, and guard heights range from 26" to 48" with no governing detail.

Top three areas of concern: (1) drawing-to-specification product and assembly conflicts, which will generate substitution RFIs and change orders at nearly every trade; (2) missing waterproofing, flashing, and drainage detailing at showers, floor drains, windows, decks and the crawl space/stem-wall transition; (3) the unresolved and undocumented life-safety package — egress openings, safety glazing, alarms, stair/guard geometry, garage separation, and pool barrier. Resolving the schedules (door 11-1, window egress and glazing data, Powder Room finish, Entry Hall and Reading Nook wall finishes) and issuing a coordinated pool/deck package should be treated as prerequisites to bidding.
