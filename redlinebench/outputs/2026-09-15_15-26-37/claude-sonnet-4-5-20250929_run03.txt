# Architectural Review: Bonfire House

## Step 1: Project Context

**Project identification:**
Bonfire House, Chatham County, North Carolina; subject to 2018 North Carolina Residential Code (adopting 2015 IRC with amendments) under Chatham County Building Inspectors and Central Permitting jurisdiction.

**Building program:**
Single-family residence, R-3 occupancy, Type V-B construction. Four bedrooms, 3.5 bathrooms, two stories above sealed crawl space. 3,270 SF heated area plus 570 SF garage; total 3,840 SF. Site includes above-ground lap pool (8' × 60'), wood deck, septic system, and private well.

**Climate:**
Zone 4A (mixed-humid).

**Document scope:**
Complete architectural drawing set (34 sheets: general, site, floor plans, elevations, sections, wall/floor assemblies, exterior/interior/deck details, schedules). Structural drawings noted as separate; MEP, civil not included. Eleven-page project specification covering materials, finishes, systems, and allowances.

## Summary

The Bonfire House documents exhibit coordination deficiencies common to residential design-bid-build projects where multiple selection and specification decisions remain open at bid time. The three most critical areas requiring resolution are:

1. **Building envelope assembly conflicts** (C-2, C-3, D-1, D-5): Wall and roof insulation specifications do not match drawn assemblies in type, thickness, or sequence. The weather barrier position in wall assembly E-4 creates potential moisture trapping, and the crawl space wall insulation thickness discrepancy (2" specified vs. 1/2" drawn) represents a four-fold thermal performance error. Roof rigid insulation is un-dimensioned despite being critical to condensation control in an unvented assembly.

2. **Code-required fire and life safety documentation gaps** (E-1 through E-5): Garage-to-house separation lacks fire-rated wall designation, fire-rated/self-closing door specification, and Type X ceiling requirement despite habitable space above. Stair geometry arithmetic fails (12" risers calculated vs. 7-3/4" maximum allowed), and emergency egress window compliance for bedrooms cannot be verified from documents provided.

3. **Material specification inconsistencies** (A-3, C-1, A-9, G-6, G-7): Window manufacturer conflict (Marvin scheduled vs. Andersen specified) creates bid ambiguity on a major visible component. Abbreviation "SS" used for both stainless steel and stone surfaces. Door hardware almost entirely unspecified beyond entry door. Shower door framing types separated between schedule and specs. These represent coordination failures between different document types that will generate immediate RFIs.

Beyond these critical issues, the documents contain numerous incomplete references ("FOR SHOWER FINISHES SEE" with no destination), TBD placeholders on cost-significant items, and missing details for constructability (pool-to-deck transition, entry hall assembly sequencing, deck ledger flashing). The drawing set is graphically thorough but requires specification alignment, completion of deferred decisions, and code compliance verification before construction start.
