## Step 1: Project Context

- **Project:** Bonfire House, Chatham County, North Carolina (unaddressed parcel, zoned Residential District 1). Code: 2018 NC Residential Code (IRC 2015 w/ amendments); Chatham County Central Permitting.
- **Building type:** New single-family residence, R-3, stated as Type V-B; 2 stories over sealed crawl space, 3,840 SF total / 3,270 SF heated, 4 bedrooms, 3.5 baths, above-ground lap pool, wood deck.
- **Climate zone:** 4A (stated on G0.11).
- **Drawing scope:** G-series (symbols, code summary, 3D views) and A-series (site, floor/roof plans, elevations, sections, assemblies, exterior/deck/interior details, RCP with lighting schedule, door/window/finish/appliance schedules). Survey removed. No structural, MEP, civil, or landscape drawings (structural repeatedly referenced as "see structural").
- **Specifications:** An 11-page narrative "Description of Materials, Applications and Allowances" dated 10/20/2021 — not CSI-formatted; no numbered divisions.

## Summary

The set is graphically rich and the assembly sheets are well-organized, but coordination between the drawings, the code summary, and the narrative specification is weak: the two documents describe different window manufacturers, different exterior wall systems (ZIP R-sheathing vs. plywood + rigid + WRB), different insulation values, different facade cladding locations, and different fixture packages. Within the drawings themselves, the Entry Hall is the most troubled area — roof slope, top-of-framing height, stud size, and header space are all shown inconsistently across five sheets — and the entry, garage, and porch roofs are all drawn below the minimum slope of the specified snap-lock metal panel. Life-safety documentation is thin: the garage ceiling separation, garage door ratings/closers, egress windows, safety glazing, pool-barrier door hardware, and exterior stair guards are either wrong or unaddressed. The top three areas of concern are (1) the roof system (slope vs. product, missing underlayment, furring attachment, entry hall geometry), (2) spec-to-drawing conflicts on envelope products and R-values that make the bid basis ambiguous, and (3) garage separation and egress/pool-barrier life-safety omissions.
