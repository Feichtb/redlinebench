## Step 1: Project Context

- **Project:** Bonfire House, Chatham County, North Carolina; 2018 NC Residential Code (IRC 2015 with amendments)
- **Building type:** Single-family residential (R-3), Construction Type V-B, 4 bedrooms, 2 stories, 3,270 SF heated + 570 SF garage
- **Climate zone:** 4A (stated in documents)
- **Drawing set scope:** General sheets (symbols, code summary, 3D views); Architectural sheets (site plan, floor plans, elevations, sections, wall/floor assemblies, exterior details, deck plans/details, reflected ceiling plan, interior elevations, door/window schedules). Survey removed from review copy. Structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, and civil drawings are not included.
- **Specifications:** 11-page project specification covering sitework, foundations, framing, exterior/interior finishes, MEP systems, appliances, pool, well, and septic

## Summary

This bid set demonstrates competent architectural documentation overall but contains several significant coordination failures that would generate RFIs and potential change orders during construction. The **three areas of greatest concern** are:

1. **Stair geometry non-compliance**: Both stair sections calculate to riser heights of 12" or more, far exceeding the 7¾" maximum. This is not a documentation error but a fundamental design problem requiring redesign before permitting.

2. **Window specification conflict**: The complete mismatch between Marvin (drawings) and Andersen (specifications) affects procurement, lead times, detailing, and energy compliance verification — this must be resolved before bidding.

3. **Life safety omissions**: The garage-to-residence separation lacks required fire ratings on the connecting door and ceiling assembly; bedroom egress windows need verification; and smoke/CO detector locations are undocumented.

Secondary concerns include incomplete product selections (TBD items), the absence of structural drawings for coordination, and constructability challenges at the custom wood-clad Entry Hall and pool deck interfaces. The documents are organized and graphically clear, but the coordination gaps between schedules, specifications, and drawn conditions indicate the set was not fully cross-checked before issuance.
