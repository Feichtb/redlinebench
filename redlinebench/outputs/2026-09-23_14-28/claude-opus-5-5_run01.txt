# Bonfire House – Construction Document Review

## Step 1: Project Context

- **Project:** Bonfire House, an unaddressed 9.39-acre parcel in Chatham County, NC. The governing code is the 2018 North Carolina Residential Code, which adopts the IRC 2015 with amendments.
- **Building type:** New two-story single-family residence, R-3 occupancy, Type V-B construction. It includes a sealed crawl space, an attached garage with a guest suite above, decks, and an above-ground lap pool.
- **Climate zone:** 4A (stated on G0.11).
- **Drawing set scope:**
  - Included: General sheets (G0.01–G1.00), site plan, crawl space, floor and roof plans, enlarged plans, exterior elevations, building and wall sections, wall and floor/roof assemblies, exterior details, deck sheets, RCP, interior elevations and details, and door/window/finish schedules.
  - Absent: Structural, MEP, and civil disciplines. The survey was removed for this review.
- **Specifications:** An 11-page outline "Description of Materials, Applications and Allowances," organized by trade topic. It is not in CSI MasterFormat divisions.

## Summary

The set is well organized and graphically clear. Many dimension strings reconcile, and the window schedule largely agrees with the plan tags. However, the specifications and drawings were evidently developed separately, with frequent product, material, location and quantity conflicts (windows, doors, cladding locations, wall sheathing, hydrants, solar scope).

The three areas of greatest concern are:

1. **Roof assembly and slopes.** The roof is unresolved: vented versus unvented, underlayment position, metal panels on furring over foam, snap-lock panels at 2:12 and 1/4:12, and main-block slopes that do not reconcile.
2. **Life safety and code coverage.** There are gaps in garage separation, emergency escape and safety glazing documentation, pool barrier and gate hardware, and a 26" stair guard.
3. **Finish and door schedules.** Inappropriate or undefined entries (carpet in the Powder Room, T-1, missing door 11-1, hardware left blank), together with extensive TBDs and open owner questions in the spec, will drive RFIs and allowance disputes during bidding.
